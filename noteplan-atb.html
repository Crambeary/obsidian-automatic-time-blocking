This feature is not compatible with AutoTimeBlocking, so you must set the "Create Events" to "None" under the cog/setting icon at the top of the calendar to use the /atb command. What is AutoTimeBlocking? Research has proven that people are more productive when they plan, schedule, and block time to work on tasks. The reverse is also true -- when you don't plan, it increases your cognitive load. It decreases your productivity to constantly remember to return to a ToDo app (or even ToDos in NotePlan) to decide what to work on. And when you open your phone to go to your ToDo app, you might get lost on Instagram by accident. 😳 Planning your day in blocks helps dramatically -- it reminds you what to do when, lowering cognitive overhead and giving you time to focus on getting things done. NotePlan has a built-in, compelling feature that allows you to block out times (temporarily) on your calendar to work on certain things. Read about Time Blocking in NotePlan As Noteplan's author, Eduard Metzger, once wrote: Time blocking is like budgeting, but for your time, not for your money. Budgeting is a tripwire helping to break out of autopilot and ask yourself if it still makes sense what you are doing. Budget your time to avoid getting into rabbit holes. 🐇 🕳 The core Noteplan time blocking functionality works great, but to do it, you have to look at your calendar, find slots that work, figure out what the start/stop time for those slots are, and type them into a note to create the blocks. This is not hard if you do it for one or two daily things. But then inevitably, things come up, and you miss your time block. Or you have more things you need to add. You need to re-edit all the times manually. That's a hassle. I'd bet you eventually give up and go back to flipping through Instagram instead. That's why I wrote the AutoTimeBlocking Plugin (|/atb|). The |/atb| plugin can take tasks that you have scheduled for today (either with the |> today| tag or by scheduling it within the app, and |/atb| will find appropriate times in the day for you to work on those tasks. AND MORE IMPORTANTLY, when something comes up during your time block (you get hungry, you get a phone call, or...) and you don't get done what you wanted to, you just rerun the command, and it finds new slots for you. As much as I try to stay inside my time blocks and get everything done, I run the command several times a day, re-syncing with my day. *It still helps me immensely to have a plan for what to work on when*. How does it work? In NotePlan, when you create a todo with the |> today| tag or today's date on the task, it shows up in the "References" section at the top of today's note. Tasks can be anywhere, in any document. And then, with AutoTimeBlocking, today’s “references” automatically become Time Blocks which NotePlan renders on your calendar, reminding you to get those tasks done and suggesting good times for doing them: Then all you have to do is pay attention to what it says you should do when and voila! Easy as that…everything gets done! ;) If it doesn’t all get done... rerun the command and block a new set of times! How do you get started? First, Learn About Time Blocking An essential first step is learning how Time Blocking works in NotePlan. If you haven't already, Read about Time Blocking -- it's a short, quick read and is essential background for what this plugin is trying to automate. However, you may not see those events immediately on your calendar if you click over to your Apple Calendar (or Fantastical, Google Calendar, or another app). That is not a NotePlan issue as much as it's an iCal refresh setting. Research has proven that people are more productive when they plan, schedule, and block time to work on tasks. The reverse is also true -- when you don't plan, it increases your cognitive load. It decreases your productivity to constantly remember to return to a ToDo app (or even ToDos in NotePlan) to decide what to work on. And when you open your phone to go to your ToDo app, you might get lost on Instagram by accident. 😳 Planning your day in blocks helps dramatically -- it reminds you what to do when, lowering cognitive overhead and giving you time to focus on getting things done. NotePlan has a built-in, compelling feature that allows you to block out times (temporarily) on your calendar to work on certain things. [Read about Time Blocking in NotePlan](https://help.noteplan.co/article/52-part-2-tasks-events-and-reminders#timeblocking) As Noteplan's author, Eduard Metzger, once wrote: > Time blocking is like budgeting, but for your time, not for your money. > Budgeting is a tripwire helping to break out of autopilot and ask yourself > if it still makes sense what you are doing. Budget your time to avoid getting > into rabbit holes. 🐇 🕳 The core Noteplan time blocking functionality works great, but to do it, you have to look at your calendar, find slots that work, figure out what the start/stop time for those slots are, and type them into a note to create the blocks. This is not hard if you do it for one or two daily things. But then inevitably, things come up, and you miss your time block. Or you have more things you need to add. You need to re-edit all the times manually. That's a hassle. I'd bet you eventually give up and go back to flipping through Instagram instead. That's why I wrote the AutoTimeBlocking Plugin (`/atb`). The `/atb` plugin can take tasks that you have scheduled for today (either with the `> today` tag or by scheduling it within the app, and `/atb` will find appropriate times in the day for you to work on those tasks. AND MORE IMPORTANTLY, when something comes up during your time block (you get hungry, you get a phone call, or...) and you don't get done what you wanted to, you just rerun the command, and it finds new slots for you. As much as I try to stay inside my time blocks and get everything done, I run the command several times a day, re-syncing with my day. **It still helps me immensely to have a plan for what to work on when**. ## How does it work? In NotePlan, when you create a todo with the `> today` tag or today's date on the task, it shows up in the "References" section at the top of today's note. Tasks can be anywhere, in any document. And then, with AutoTimeBlocking, today’s “references” automatically become Time Blocks which NotePlan renders on your calendar, reminding you to get those tasks done and suggesting good times for doing them: ![image](AutoTimeBlocking%20Readme_attachments/Screenshot%2011-28-21%20at%2010.57.43%20AM.png) Then all you have to do is pay attention to what it says you should do when and voila! Easy as that…everything gets done! ;) If it doesn’t all get done... rerun the command and block a new set of times! How do you get started? ## First, Learn About Time Blocking An essential first step is learning how Time Blocking works in NotePlan. If you haven't already, [Read about Time Blocking](https://help.noteplan.co/article/52-part-2-tasks-events-and-reminders#timeblocking) -- it's a short, quick read and is essential background for what this plugin is trying to automate. ## Launching the Plugin - Open up Today's Calendar Note (CMD-T) - Invoke the AutoTimeBlocking plugin by typing: `/atb` - You should see it create time blocks in your Today calendar note for **todo items which are in your References section** ## So, What's It Supposed To Do? - The `/atb` plugin pulls items from the References section in the document and helps you create Time Blocks for them. Any items in References that are completed tasks are ignored. - Tasks and other notes can live anywhere in NotePlan (Project Notes, Calendar Notes, etc.), and they show up in the References section in Today's Calendar Note if they are tagged with > today or have today's date in the task line (e.g.,>2021-11-27) - The `/atb` plugin will try to find time and make a Time Block for each item in your References section (things you said you wanted to do today) - NOTE: As you complete an item you created a time block for, mark it completed in the References section so if you run the /atb command again, it will know to ignore that item and won't create a new time block for it - TODAY-ONLY LIMITATION: AutoTimeBlocking can only be run today for today's Time Blocks, because a task marked for > today is only relevant on today's note. There is currently no way to create AutoTimeBlocks for tomorrow or the future -- see the Roadmap at the bottom of this document for some ideas for future features/improvements. Note, however, that you can create Synced Lines for future Calendar Note dates (if a task is flagged for >thatDate). - `/mdatb - Mark Done and run ATB` - This command is a shortcut to mark all selected *tasks* as ***done*** and automatically run /atb to tell you what to work on next. It's just a shortcut for marking a task and then running the `/atb` command. Note: you can highlight multiple tasks and text/titles etc. and it will only set the selected lines that are *tasks (of any type)* as done. ## When In The Day Are Time Blocks Placed? - By default, Time Blocks can be created all day long. You can also change the settings, for instance, to only allow Time Blocks, for example, between `08:00` and `18:00` by changing the "work day" times in Settings (see below) - Obviously, time blocks are not placed when they would overlap with events on your calendar or items you've time blocked by hand ## Setting Task/Block Length - By default, tasks get 15-minute length Time Blocks. If you have tasks that are not 15-mins, you can set the estimated length by entering the length when you create or edit the task, like this (using the apostrophe and an `h` for an hour and `m` for minutes anywhere in the task: `* Do something that takes an hour and a half '1h30m` (1 hour, 30 min task) - Note: you can change the apostrophe to another character in settings (see below) ## Prioritizing Tasks/Time Blocks (aka Sorting) - By default, the plugin sorts items in ascending order of time duration. Getting small tasks done is rewarding and builds momentum, so the plugin will try to insert as many smaller tasks as possible (pebbles) onto the larger ones (the boulders). See below for how to override this behavior. - If you have tasks you want to be sorted as a higher priority, you can put exclamation points (e.g. '! '!! or '!!!) anywhere in the task line. The more exclamations you give, the higher the priority. So, a task like: `* Something important '1h45m !` - ...will be a higher priority than all tasks with no exclamation marks, so the plugin will try to find time for it first, and a task with two `!!` will come before that one (assuming there is a time slot that it fits in). ## Settings - The plugin runs perfectly well without ever seeing or changing the Settings. However, suppose you want to fine-tune your experience. In that case, you can change the plugin's behavior through the settings panel on the NotePlan Mac desktop app (configuration is not currently possible on iOS). - To open up the settings panel go to NotePlan > Preferences>> Plugins, and click the cog/gear icon next to the "Event Automations" plugin ![image](AutoTimeBlocking%20Readme_attachments/Screen%20Shot%202022-02-21%20at%204.09.07%20PM.png) ## Here's what each of those settings means: ### "Default time block duration" (default: 15; presetKey: "defaultDuration") - The default duration for an event is 15 minutes. That means if no duration marker/string is explicitly typed in a task, it will assume the task takes 15m. Change this to any value you want for your default task duration. This setting allows you to not worry about entering times for common short tasks. If no time is specified, the task is assumed to be '15m. ### "Include all todos in today's note" (default: true; presetKey: "includeAllTodos") - Also include todos written in today's note (assume they are implicitly dated to be done today) ### "Checking a checklist item marks the original item done" (default: true; presetKey: "checkedItemChecksOriginal") - Checking off a checklist AutoTimeBlocked line item will find and check the original task. To make this work, the onEditorWillSave trigger/frontmatter will be automatically added by the plugin.\nIMPORTANT NOTE: Only applies if:\n(1) you use the “+” for your Time Block Leading Character and\n s(2) the 'Include links to task location in time blocks' is set to 'Pretty Links' ### "Leave completed timeblocks when re-calculating?" (default: false; presetKey: "leaveCompletedTBs") - When enabled, timeblocks that have been marked complete will remain in the note/calendar when timeblocks are re-calculated. The plugin removes the AutoTimeBlock tag from completed items so they are not deleted during the next timeblock calculation, preserving your completed work for posterity. ### "Create a link from the time block to the original task" (default: "Pretty Links"; presetKey: "includeLinks") - Add a link to the original task so you can easily get back to it to make notes - These links can be either [[wiki links]] or url-type links - Note: if you want checked timeblocks to automatically check the underlying item, this setting should be set to: 'Pretty Links' ### "Allow tasks to be split" (default: false; presetKey: "allowEventSplits") - By default, tasks only are placed in Time Blocks that are equal to their full length. So, if you have a `'2h` task but there are no open two-hour blocks in your day, that task will not be placed. If you change this setting to `true (checked)`, it will allow the plugin to split tasks, and so when it gets to this `'2h` task in the priority waterfall, it will grab any free block time it can find, even if that means creating eight 15-minute blocks throughout the day to get this task its entire two hours. Or, if there are not enough slots to get the whole two hours, it will take as many as possible. ### "Include any tasks that match text" (default: empty -- match all referenced tasks; presetKey: "includeTasksWithText") - If you put text in this list (separated by commas), you can limit the items that /atb includes in time blocks to these items. For instance, if you enter: `blox,tbx` then only tasks with the words 'blox' OR 'tbx' anywhere in the task would be included. So: `* this one should be included #blox` `* this one should be included tbx` `* this one would not be included.` - Pro tip: If you know Regular Expressions (RegExp), you can enter a regex in the place of a string (e.g. `box,tbx,/^f/`) would also add any tasks which start with the letter "f" to the previous list ### "Exclude any tasks that match text" (default: empty -- do not exclude anything; presetKey: "excludeTasksWithText") - If you put text strings in this array, you can exclude items that /atb includes in time blocks to these items. For instance, if you enter: `excludeTasksWithText: blox, tbx,` then tasks with the words 'blox' OR 'tbx' anywhere in the task would be EXCLUDED. So: `* this one should be excluded #blox` `* this one should be excluded tbx` `* this one would be included.` - Pro tip: If you know Regular Expressions (RegExp), you can enter a regex in the place of a string (e.g. `box, tbx, /^f/`), which would also exclude any tasks which start with the letter "f" to the previous list ### "Task duration marker" (default: `'`; presetKey: "durationMarker") - Any unique marker can signify the start of a task duration string in your task text. By default, it's an apostrophe (e.g. `'2h32m`), but if you changed `durationMarker` to "~", you could enter `~2h32m`, and it would work just the same. ### "Time block leading character" (default: `*`; presetKey: "todoChar") - This is the character/sequence used when the Time Blocks are created in the Calendar Note. There are only a few characters that NotePlan will recognize as the beginning of a Time Block line. The default for the plugin is a star (basically a ToDo, because that's (currently) the only character that will work with writing the time blocks permanently to the calendar). Other legal options for this character are: `-`, or `#` (headings, which could be #, ##, ###, or ####) . @codedungeon likes his TimeBlocks to be #### headings, but as stated above, only the `*` currently works if you want to turn your time blocks into full-fledged calendar entries ### "Unique AutoTimeBlock tag" (default: "#🕑"; presetKey: "timeBlockTag") - This tag is placed by the plugin on the Time Blocks it creates in the Calendar Note. This differentiates Time Blocks created by the plugin from any others that may have been hand-written by a user. This way, the plugin can delete and recreate Time Blocks as the day progresses. This tag also is attached to calendar events created by the plugin. This tag can be any text value. See `createCalendarEntries` in the Settings section. - Note: You can use NotePlan's Theme API/settings to hide this tag from view in the Editor. So, even though the plugin will put this tag on a Time Block line, your theme settings could hide that tag, so you don't see it. See `Hiding TimeBlockTag in your Theme` below ### Remove Duration (default: true/checked; presetKey: "removeDuration") - The plugin assumes that your duration markers, e.g. `'2h32m, are only there for Auto creating the TimeBlock, so that number is removed when the Time Block is created, so you don't clutter the calendar text. If you want to keep the durations string in your Time Block or Calendar Event (see below), change this value to false ### "Heading for time blocks in note" (default: "Time Blocks" -- note *no* hashes; presetKey: "timeBlockHeading") - Time Blocks are by default placed at the top of Today's Calendar Note under a title heading specified by this setting. However, suppose there is a pre-existing heading with the name specified in the timeBlockHeading field somewhere in the note. In that case, the plugin will place the generated Time Blocks under that existing heading. This is an excellent way to keep your Daily Notes organized using Templates and Daily Notes. Just put a header like `## Time Blocks` in your template, and the `/atb` plugin will set the Time Blocks under that heading. - If you want your time blocks to be inserted naked into the top of the note without any heading, make this timeBlockHeading an empty string, e.g. `timeBlockHeading: '',` ### "Start of work day" (default: "00:00"; presetKey: "workDayStart") - The plugin will only start looking to place blocks at or after the `workDayStart` time AND after the time it is right now. Note: you must use 24-hour/military time for these times. AM/PM etc., do not work.
